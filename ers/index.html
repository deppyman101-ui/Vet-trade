What ERS does

One organised point of contact.

After a death, a property can leave an executor or family coordinating several different jobs at once. ERS manages the practical process so you do not have to source and chase every provider yourself.

Common questions

Before you instruct ERS.

If you are unsure whether your situation fits, request an assessment and we can establish what ERS can appropriately help with.

Do I need Grant of Probate before contacting ERS?

No. You can contact ERS earlier. However, the authority required depends on what you want us to do. We may be able to assess and plan before some later actions are authorised, but sale, donation, disposal or other substantive decisions require appropriate authority.

Will ERS just clear everything from the property?

No. A key part of the model is separating the assessment from disposal. Potentially significant, sentimental or disputed items can be recorded and held for a client decision before authorised clearance takes place.

Does ERS value antiques or specialist items?

ERS identifies items that may warrant specialist attention and coordinates appropriate independent valuers, auction houses or specialist buyers where required. ERS itself does not provide formal probate valuation advice.

Who pays third-party contractors?

Third-party costs are normally quoted separately and approved before commitment. The intended operating model is for contractors or specialist providers to invoice the estate or authorised client directly where appropriate, with ERS coordinating and recording the work.

Can a solicitor refer a client directly?

Yes. Solicitors and other appropriate professional referrers can introduce an executor or authorised representative to ERS. The client remains in control of the practical instructions and approvals.
